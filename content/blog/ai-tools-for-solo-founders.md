Being a solo founder means doing the work of a whole team. AI tools can cover a surprising amount of that: writing code, producing marketing assets, automating admin and even answering support calls. The risk is spending more time trying tools than building. This guide gives you a lean, practical stack by job, using tools from our rankings, plus advice on what not to automate.

## Building the product

- **Non-technical founder:** start with an AI app builder. [Lovable](/tools/lovable.html) builds full-stack apps with Supabase built in; [Replit](/tools/replit.html) combines agent, editor and hosting. See [best AI tools for beginners to build apps](/blog/best-ai-tools-for-beginners-to-build-apps.html).
- **Technical founder:** an AI editor like [Cursor](/tools/cursor.html) plus a terminal agent like [Claude Code](/tools/claude-code.html) or [OpenAI Codex](/tools/openai-codex.html) can multiply your output. See [best AI code editor 2026](/blog/best-ai-code-editor-2026.html).
- **UI and landing pages:** [v0](/tools/v0.html) generates polished interfaces quickly.

For a full roadmap, read [how to build a SaaS with AI](/blog/how-to-build-a-saas-with-ai.html).

## Backend, hosting and AI features

- **Database and auth:** [Supabase](/tools/supabase.html) covers most needs in one service.
- **Hosting:** [Vercel](/tools/vercel.html) for Next.js; [Render](/tools/render.html) for servers and workers.
- **AI inside your product:** [OpenRouter](/tools/openrouter.html) lets you try many models with one key; the [Gemini API](/tools/google-gemini-api.html) has a free tier for prototyping. Read the [AI API pricing guide](/blog/ai-api-pricing-guide.html).

## Automating operations

[n8n](/tools/n8n.html) lets you build workflows visually and self-host for free: route new sign-ups to your CRM, summarise feedback, post release notes, or triage emails with an AI node. Start with one painful, repetitive task and automate only that. If you outgrow visual workflows, see [how to build an AI agent](/blog/how-to-build-an-ai-agent.html).

Keep a human in the loop for anything customer-facing or irreversible, such as sending emails, issuing refunds or deleting data. Automation should prepare actions; you approve them.

## Content and marketing

- **Writing:** AI assistants are useful for outlines, first drafts and repurposing one piece into many formats. Edit for your voice and verify every fact; never publish invented statistics or testimonials.
- **Video:** [Runway](/tools/runway.html), [Kling AI](/tools/kling-ai.html) and [Google Veo (Flow)](/tools/google-veo-flow.html) for b-roll and short clips; [HeyGen](/tools/heygen.html) for presenter-style explainers and translations. Always show your real product for demos. See [best AI video generators](/blog/best-ai-video-generators.html).
- **Voice:** [ElevenLabs](/tools/elevenlabs.html) for narration. See [best AI voice tools](/blog/best-ai-voice-tools.html).

## Customer support

A good help centre plus an AI assistant that answers from your docs can handle common questions, with clear escalation to you. For phone support or booking, voice platforms like [Vapi](/tools/vapi.html) can run a narrow, well-scoped agent. Always tell users when they are talking to AI and make reaching a human easy.

## A lean starter stack

1. **Build:** Lovable or Cursor.
2. **Backend:** Supabase.
3. **Host:** Vercel.
4. **Automate:** n8n (self-hosted).
5. **Content:** an AI assistant for drafts plus one video tool.

All have free tiers or free self-hosting to start. Upgrade only the tool that is limiting you.

## What not to hand to AI

- **Talking to customers.** Interviews and sales calls build the insight your product needs.
- **Final decisions** on pricing, positioning and what to build next.
- **Security and data responsibility.** Use AI to review, but you own the outcome; see the [security checklist](/blog/vibe-coding-security-checklist.html).
- **Your voice.** Audiences can tell when everything is generic.

## Avoiding tool sprawl

Every tool adds cost, logins and context switching. Keep a short list of what you use and why, review it monthly, and cancel anything you have not used in weeks. A focused founder with five tools usually ships faster than one with twenty.

## Weekly rhythm that works

Many solo founders split the week: a few deep-work blocks for building with AI agents, one block for customer conversations, one for content, and a short review of metrics and automations. AI makes each block more productive, but the rhythm keeps you from building in a vacuum.

## Measuring whether a tool is worth it

For each tool, ask one question after a month: what did it let me ship or stop doing? If you cannot name a concrete outcome, such as a feature shipped faster, hours of admin removed or content published, it is a candidate to cancel. Track this in a simple list next to each subscription's renewal date. Tools that pass this test earn their place; the rest are distractions, however impressive their demos.

Start small, measure honestly, and let your stack grow only as your product does.

## FAQ

### What AI tools do solo founders need?
A builder or AI editor, a backend like Supabase, hosting, one automation tool and one content tool cover most needs.

### Can AI replace a co-founder?
AI can cover much of the execution work, but not judgement, customer relationships or accountability. Many solo founders lean on advisors and communities for those.

### How much should I spend on AI tools at the start?
As little as possible. Use free tiers until a tool clearly limits your progress, then upgrade that one tool.
