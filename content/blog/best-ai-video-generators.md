AI video has gone from novelty to a practical tool for product demos, social clips, ads and explainers. But "AI video" covers very different jobs: generating cinematic clips from a prompt is not the same as producing a talking-head explainer in five languages. This guide maps the tools in our [Video rankings](/category/video.html) to the jobs they do best. We do not quote prices or credit counts, which change often; check each official site.

## Two kinds of AI video tools

1. **Generative video models** create new footage from text or images: [Runway](/tools/runway.html), [Google Veo via Flow](/tools/google-veo-flow.html) and [Kling AI](/tools/kling-ai.html).
2. **Avatar and presenter tools** create a person speaking your script, often with translation and lip-sync: [HeyGen](/tools/heygen.html).

Decide which job you have before comparing tools.

## Runway: the creator favourite

Runway offers pro-grade generation alongside a suite of editing tools, so you can generate, extend and edit clips in one place. It is popular with creators and filmmakers who want control rather than one-shot results. There is a free tier to try it.

Best for: creative work, stylised clips and creators who want generation plus editing.

## Google Veo (Flow): high quality with native audio

Google's Veo models, available through the Flow filmmaking tool, produce high-quality clips and can generate native audio alongside video. Access depends on your Google AI plan, so check what your subscription includes.

Best for: realistic, polished short clips, especially when you want sound generated with the video.

## Kling AI: strong motion at a good price

Kling AI is known for strong motion quality for short clips and offers free credits to start. It is a good value option when you need many short generations, such as social content variations.

Best for: short clips with lots of movement, and volume experimentation.

## HeyGen: talking avatars and translated videos

HeyGen focuses on presenter-style videos: an avatar (stock or a custom one of yourself, with consent) delivers your script. Its video translation features can dub and lip-sync existing videos into other languages. There is a free tier.

Best for: product explainers, onboarding videos, sales outreach and localisation.

## Practical workflow tips

- **Storyboard first.** Write shot-by-shot prompts: subject, action, camera movement, lighting, style.
- **Generate short, then stitch.** Short clips are more reliable than long ones; assemble them in an editor.
- **Use image-to-video for consistency.** Start from a reference image to keep characters and products consistent between shots.
- **Budget credits.** Expect several generations per usable shot.
- **Add voice separately if needed.** For narration, see [the best AI voice tools](/blog/best-ai-voice-tools.html).

## Rights and responsibility

Read each tool's terms on commercial use. Only create avatars or voice clones of people who have given consent, and label AI-generated content where platforms or laws require it.

## FAQ

### What is the best AI video generator?
For creative control, Runway. For realistic clips with audio, Veo via Flow. For value on short clips, Kling. For talking-head explainers, HeyGen.

### Are there free AI video generators?
Runway, Kling and HeyGen offer free tiers or credits. Veo access depends on your Google AI plan.

### Can I use AI video commercially?
Often yes on paid plans, but terms vary. Check each tool's terms of service.
